# Lintel & Lime: buyer research (desk study)

**[Visit the website](https://lintel-and-lime.netlify.app)** · [Case study](case-study.md) · [All concepts](../../README.md)

*Fictional interior design and renovation studio, portfolio demonstration.*

This is desk research, not user research: published guides, news and studies about how Indian homeowners buy interior work. Most cost figures come from designer and vendor blogs, which have commercial interests, so they are used as **rough ranges for a fictional estimator**, not market data. Each finding is an assumption to test with a real studio and its clients.

## Findings

### 1. Prices vary widely, so visitors can't judge a quote (Moderate)

Published per-square-foot ranges for full home interiors:

| Source type | Range (₹ per sq ft) |
|---|---|
| National guides | about 1,000 to 3,500 |
| Delhi 2BHK guide | budget 1,800 to 2,200; mid 2,500 to 3,500; luxury 4,000 to 6,000+ |
| Hyderabad guide | about 1,200 to 2,800 |
| Renovation (structural and finishing) | about 1,200 to 6,000+ |
| Modular kitchen | about 1,300 to 3,000 (higher with tiling and plumbing) |
| Wardrobes | about 1,200 (laminate) to 2,500+ (acrylic) |

One guide says kitchens and wardrobes take 60 to 70% of a typical interior budget. One source says interior work and design fees attract 18% GST and many quotes leave it out. **Check this against current GST rules before using it for a real client.**

**For the site:** a range of 3 to 5 times between budget and luxury is exactly why visitors can't tell what they'll pay. An estimator with clear finish levels, and with GST shown separately, answers the first question every visitor has.

### 2. The fear is unfinished work, overruns and hidden exclusions (Moderate)

- A June 2026 news report describes Mumbai homeowners alleging that fit-out firms took large advances and left projects unfinished. That's anecdotal, but it's the fear made concrete.
- Indian construction studies name scope creep, delays, rework and awarding work to the lowest bidder as the main causes of cost overruns.
- A vendor blog says low quotes often leave out materials, labour or services, and the cost appears later.
- One guide gives 45 to 75 days to build a 1 to 2 BHK after the design is approved.

**For the site:** state what's included and excluded, show a timeline, and **tie payments to finished stages**, not to the calendar or a large upfront advance.

### 3. Payments by stage are common, but there's no standard (Weak to moderate)

Published structures vary. A typical pattern: about 10% to book and for concept design, payments as detailed design and material choices are finished, about 40% during site work, and **about 10% held until handover**. One guide advises holding the last 10% through a short defect check after handover. Interior design isn't a regulated profession in India, so the contract is what protects the client.

**For the site:** show the payment schedule next to the process, with no large advance, and the final payment after a snag list is closed.

## What this means for the site

| Visitor's doubt | Section or feature |
|---|---|
| "What will my home cost?" | **Cost estimator**: room, size, finish level → price range and timeline, GST shown separately |
| "What's the catch?" | **Included and not included**, written out plainly |
| "Will you finish? Will you run late?" | **Process with timelines**, and **payments tied to finished stages** |
| "Will it look like the 3D render?" | **Render compared with the finished room** |
| "Have you done homes like mine?" | **Projects with real numbers**: size, budget range, duration, what was done |
| "What do the finish levels mean?" | **Finish levels explained** with the actual materials |
| "Is my budget enough for you?" | The estimator sets expectations before anyone calls, so enquiries arrive already qualified |

## Assumptions to test with a real studio

1. Price uncertainty is the main reason Instagram followers don't enquire.
2. Showing price ranges filters out mismatched enquiries without scaring off good ones.
3. Payments tied to finished stages make visitors noticeably more confident.
4. A render shown next to the finished room is more convincing than finished photos alone.
